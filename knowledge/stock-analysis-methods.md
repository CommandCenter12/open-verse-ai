# Stock Analysis Methods

## Price to earnings ratio (P/E)
The P/E ratio is share price divided by earnings per share (EPS). Trailing P/E uses the last 12 months of reported earnings; forward P/E uses analyst estimates. A high P/E can mean investors expect growth or that the stock is expensive. Compare P/E only within the same industry, and note it is meaningless when earnings are negative.

## Earnings per share (EPS)
EPS is net income minus preferred dividends, divided by the weighted average number of common shares outstanding. Diluted EPS also counts shares that options and convertible securities could create.

## Price to book ratio (P/B)
P/B is market price per share divided by book value per share, where book value is total assets minus total liabilities. It is most useful for asset-heavy businesses such as banks and less useful for companies whose value is in intangible assets.

## Moving averages
A simple moving average (SMA) is the mean of the last N closing prices. An exponential moving average (EMA) weights recent prices more, using the multiplier 2 / (N + 1). The 50-day and 200-day moving averages are widely watched trend measures. A golden cross is when the 50-day crosses above the 200-day; a death cross is the opposite.

## Relative strength index (RSI)
RSI measures momentum on a 0 to 100 scale: RSI = 100 − 100 / (1 + RS), where RS is average gain divided by average loss over the lookback period, usually 14 periods. Readings above 70 are conventionally called overbought and below 30 oversold. In strong trends RSI can stay overbought or oversold for a long time.

## MACD
The MACD line is the 12-period EMA minus the 26-period EMA. The signal line is a 9-period EMA of the MACD line, and the histogram is MACD minus signal. Crossovers of the MACD and signal lines are used as momentum signals.

## Volatility
Volatility is the standard deviation of returns. To annualize daily volatility, multiply it by the square root of 252, the approximate number of trading days in a year. Higher volatility means a wider range of likely outcomes, not necessarily a higher return.

## Beta
Beta measures how a stock moves relative to the market: beta equals the covariance of the stock's returns with the market's returns divided by the variance of the market's returns. A beta of 1 moves with the market, above 1 amplifies market moves, and below 1 dampens them.

## Sharpe ratio
The Sharpe ratio is the portfolio return minus the risk-free rate, divided by the standard deviation of the portfolio's returns. It measures return per unit of total risk. The Sortino ratio is similar but divides by downside deviation only.

## Maximum drawdown
Maximum drawdown is the largest peak-to-trough decline in value before a new peak, expressed as a percentage of the peak. It shows the worst loss an investor would have experienced over the period.

## Dividend yield
Dividend yield is annual dividends per share divided by price per share. A yield that is unusually high compared with peers can signal that the market expects a dividend cut. The payout ratio, dividends divided by earnings, shows how sustainable the dividend is.

## Position sizing by risk
Fixed-fractional position sizing risks a set percentage of account equity per trade. Shares to buy equals (account equity × risk percent) divided by (entry price − stop price). For example, risking 1% of a 10,000 account with entry 50 and stop 48 gives 100 / 2 = 50 shares.
